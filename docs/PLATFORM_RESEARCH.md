# From one sport to many: can we build a "add a sport, get an evaluator" platform?

Research note, October 2026. Written for the product owner. It builds on what we learned from the
water-ski slalom demo (`README.md`, `docs/MODEL_LANDSCAPE.md`, `cvdemo/`).

---

## Executive summary (the bottom line)

1. **Yes, it is feasible, but it is a toolkit plus a method, not a magic box.** About 70 % of the code
   can be shared across sports: video ingest, detection, tracking, 2D pose, camera-motion compensation,
   an intermediate "tracks + keypoints" data format, a rule engine, a scoring layer, an annotation and
   evaluation harness, and deployment. The other 30 % is sport-specific in every new sport: which objects
   matter, which geometry (court, course, bar path), which events and phases, and what "correct" means.
   Our slalom work shows the 30 % is where most of the effort goes.
2. **"No training at all" works for a real class of activities.** These are single-person,
   human-body-centred activities filmed reasonably well: gym exercises, rep counting, joint-angle form
   checks, posture, simple drills. Off-the-shelf COCO-trained detection and pose models (YOLO11/YOLO26,
   RTMPose, ViTPose) are good enough for these. The user writes criteria ("elbow angle > 160° at
   lockout") and the platform runs them.
3. **Training becomes necessary as soon as the sport depends on a small or unusual object, an unusual
   body, or a subtle judgement.** Examples are balls, buoys, rackets, bars and ski tips; inverted
   gymnasts, swimmers and skiers in spray; and "how good was this dive". The fine-tuning is usually
   cheap: a small detector or keypoint head, trained on a few hundred to a few thousand frames that
   foundation models label automatically and a human corrects. That is exactly what we did for the
   buoys.
4. **Criteria should be code that people can read, generated by an LLM and checked against a small
   labelled set.** The user writes the criterion in plain language. An LLM turns it into a Python
   predicate over a fixed feature API (angles, distances, velocities, phases, relations). The platform
   runs it on 20–50 labelled clips and shows where it agrees and where it doesn't. A human approves it.
   This gives the speed of "describe it in English" with the reliability and explainability of
   hand-written rules.
5. **Do not let a vision-language model (VLM) be the judge.** A 2026 study found Gemini, Qwen-VL and
   InternVL only slightly above chance at action-quality scoring
   ([summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.08294)). Use VLMs for set-up help: naming objects,
   drafting rules, labelling, and describing a clip for a human. Don't use them for the 90/100 number.
6. **Scores like "90/100" need ground truth to mean anything.** For every sport, plan to collect a gold
   set of 50–200 clips scored by an expert. Without it you cannot tell a user whether the score is right.
   This was our biggest gap on slalom.
7. **Recommended MVP:** 10–14 weeks for 2–3 engineers. Validate generality on three deliberately
   different cases: **squat form** (no training, pose rules), **water-ski slalom** (port of what
   exists, small-object detector plus geometry), and **one ball sport such as tennis serve or basketball
   free throw** (ball detector plus object-person relations). Running cost is about **$0.02–0.05 per
   minute of video** on a cloud T4, before engineering and storage.

## 1. What the platform consists of

### 1.1 Layered architecture

```
 video ──► [L0 Ingest]  decode, shot/cut detection, frame sampling, metadata
             │
             ▼
          [L1 Perception primitives]  (sport-agnostic models, pluggable)
             person/object detection · open-vocab detection · tracking + ReID
             2D pose (body, hands, feet) · optional 3D pose / SMPL body
             segmentation (SAM 2/3) · camera motion (flow+RANSAC homography)
             scene registration (court/field/course keypoints → homography)
             │
             ▼
          [L2 Intermediate representation]  ← the key design decision
             per-frame, per-track: bbox, class, keypoints+conf, mask (opt),
             image coords AND registered coords (court/world/stabilised),
             camera transform per frame, shot id
             │
             ▼
          [L3 Features & events]  angles, distances, velocities, phases (rep start/bottom/top),
             relations (A left of B, A crosses line L), contact/release events
             │
             ▼
          [L4 Criteria & scoring]  rules (Python/DSL), learned classifiers, aggregation
             to a score per rep/attempt/run, with explanations + confidence
             │
             ▼
          [L5 Evaluation & annotation]  gold sets, label tool, metrics vs judges,
             active-learning queue, regression tests per sport
             │
             ▼
          [L6 Delivery]  batch API, overlay renderer, report, (later) real-time / edge
```

### 1.2 What is generic and what is sport-specific

| Layer | Sport-agnostic (build once) | Sport-specific (per sport "pack") |
|---|---|---|
| L0 Ingest | decoding, cut detection, sampling | which camera views are valid (we skip shore replays on slalom) |
| L1 Perception | person detector, tracker, 2D body pose, SAM, camera-motion compensation, open-vocab labeller | **small/unusual objects** (buoy, ball, racket, bar), extra keypoints (ski tip, club head), scene template (court lines, buoy layout) |
| L2 Representation | schema, storage (Parquet/Arrow), visualiser | which tracks/roles exist (skier, boat; server, receiver) |
| L3 Features | angle/distance/velocity library, smoothing, phase segmentation, spatial relations | which features matter, phase definitions |
| L4 Criteria | rule engine, LLM rule-authoring loop, classifier trainer, score aggregation | the criteria and weights themselves |
| L5 Evaluation | label tool, metrics, dashboards, active learning | the gold set, the official scoring rules |
| L6 Delivery | API, renderer, reports | report wording, the coach's vocabulary |

The slalom code already follows this split roughly. `pose.py`, `buoys.py` (tracker) and `camera.py` are
L1. `geometry.py`, `course.py` and `course3.py` are L3/L4 and sport-specific. The platform job is mostly
to **formalise L2 and L3** so the next sport only writes a pack.

### 1.3 Lessons from slalom that shape the design

- **Models were not the bottleneck; geometry and camera motion were.** The platform must therefore ship
  strong generic geometry tools: stabilisation, homography to a template, line-crossing tests, and
  "projected position while occluded".
- **Prefer 2D plus a registered plane over full 3D metric reconstruction.** A full metric camera model
  was fragile on broadcast footage. Most judging questions are relative ("left of", "above", "straight")
  and can be answered in image space or on a plane fitted to the scene. Make 3D optional.
- **Visibility is a first-class output.** Our pass verdict is right 87 % of the time *when seen*, but only
  ~53 % of passes are seen. Every rule must be able to return `pass / fail / unknown` with a reason.
  A platform that forces a yes/no will look confident and be wrong.

## 2. Criteria as code or config: how users define "correct"

### 2.1 The feature API is the product

Whatever authoring method we pick, it compiles to calls on a small, well-tested feature library over
L2. For example:

```python
angle(t, "r_shoulder", "r_elbow", "r_wrist")      # degrees, NaN if a keypoint is low-confidence
dist(t, "l_ankle", "r_ankle", norm="torso")        # scale-invariant
vel(t, "r_wrist", frame=t, space="court")          # px/s or m/s if registered
phase(track, "rep", detector=minima("hip_y"))      # temporal segmentation into reps
relation(track_a, obj_b, "left_of", at=event("closest_approach"))
crosses(track, line=gate("entry"), between=(t0, t1))
```

A criterion is then a predicate or a score function over one segment (a rep, an attempt, a buoy pass):

```python
@criterion("arm straight at lockout", applies_to="rep", weight=0.3)
def arm_straight(rep):
    a = rep.at("top").angle("r_shoulder", "r_elbow", "r_wrist")
    return Score.linear(a, bad=150, good=170)   # 0..1, or Unknown if not visible
```

### 2.2 Five ways to author criteria

| Approach | How it works | Strengths | Weaknesses | Accuracy expectation | Cost / latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Hand-written rules (Python)** | Engineer writes predicates like the one above | Exact, explainable, debuggable, zero data needed | Needs an engineer; thresholds tuned by hand; brittle to camera angle | High for geometric criteria (angles, sides, counts) when pose is visible; our slalom pass logic is ~87 % when seen | ms per clip on CPU |
| **B. Declarative DSL / YAML** | Non-engineers fill templates: `angle(shoulder,elbow,wrist) at phase=top > 160` | Safe, editable in a UI, versionable | Expressiveness ceiling; gets complex fast (temporal logic, occlusion); you end up building a language | Same as A for what it can express | ms |
| **C. LLM-generated rule code** | User writes "arm straight at release"; an LLM writes a Python predicate against the feature API; the platform runs it on labelled clips and reports agreement; human approves | Fast to author, keeps A's explainability; the LLM is good at the boilerplate (phases, smoothing, NaN handling) | LLM may misread intent or choose wrong thresholds, so **validation on labelled clips is mandatory**; needs a sandbox | Converges to A after 1–3 iterations if 20–50 labelled clips exist | seconds to author, ms to run |
| **D. Learned classifier on pose sequences** | Small temporal model (1D-CNN/GRU/TCN or ST-GCN) or kNN on pose embeddings, trained on labelled reps | Captures subtle "looks wrong" patterns that are hard to verbalise; robust to small noise | Needs 100s of labelled examples per class; less explainable; can learn camera bias | Good for "is this rep clean vs faulty" with enough data; Fitness-AQA shows subtle errors are hard (e.g. knees-in F1 ≈ 0.44, shallow squat F1 ≈ 0.87, [GYMetricPose](https://research.tec.mx/vivo-tec/display/AcademicArticleSCO_85200471559)) | ms on CPU |
| **E. VLM judges the clip directly** | Send frames + criteria to Gemini/GPT/Qwen-VL | Zero set-up, handles anything describable | Poor at fine motion and precise geometry; not reproducible; slow and costly per clip; can't say *why* in measurable terms | Near chance on AQA in a 2026 study ([Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2604.08294](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.08294)); 30–45 % on sport action recognition ([ActionAtlas](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05774v1)); <50 % on fine-grained motion ([FAVOR-Bench](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d2a1e47f7dc635fac77fbd6e2ec799e4-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)) | seconds and cents per clip |

### 2.3 Our recommendation

- **Default to C on top of A.** The rule is code, an LLM drafts it, and labelled clips validate it. Every
  generated rule gets a test file with its labelled clips, so it becomes a regression test. I found no
  published system doing exactly this for pose criteria; it is an engineering pattern, not a proven
  research result. It is low-risk because the output is ordinary, reviewable code.
- **Offer B only as a thin UI layer** that compiles to A. Don't design a language up front.
- **Add D when a sport has data**: hundreds of expert-labelled reps, or a sport where experts can label
  but not explain. Feed it the same L3 features, not raw pixels, so it stays small and portable.
- **Use E (VLMs) around the edges.** Use them to name objects for the auto-labeller, propose candidate
  criteria from a coaching manual, write the natural-language feedback from the rule results, and
  triage clips. Do not use them for the score.

### 2.4 From criteria to "90/100"

A score is an **aggregation** of criterion results. Make it explicit:

- Each criterion returns a value in [0,1] or `Unknown`, plus evidence (frame, measured value).
- Per-segment score = weighted mean over *known* criteria, with a coverage figure ("scored on 4 of 5
  criteria").
- Deductions-style sports (diving, gymnastics, slalom buoy counts) should mirror the official rulebook:
  deductions, buoy counts, difficulty × execution. Then the output can be compared with judges.
- Calibrate the weights against expert scores (Section 4) once a gold set exists. Before that, show the
  number as uncalibrated.

## 3. When fine-tuning is needed, and of what

### 3.1 Decision table

| Situation | Fine-tune? | What | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person in normal pose, decent resolution (gym, running, most court sports) | **No** | – | COCO person + pose are trained on exactly this |
| Small or rare sport objects: ball, buoy, puck, shuttlecock, racket, bar, gate | **Yes, usually** | Detector (YOLO11/YOLO26, RF-DETR) | Not in COCO, or in COCO but too small or blurred ("sports ball" fails at 5–15 px). Open-vocab models are too slow and miss tiny objects (our finding: Grounding DINO ~22× slower; YOLO-World missed buoys) |
| Unusual poses or appearance: inverted gymnasts, swimmers half submerged, skiers in spray, divers tucked, heavy occlusion by equipment | **Often** | Pose model (RTMPose / ViTPose / YOLO-pose) | COCO has few upside-down or partly submerged bodies; keypoints flip left/right or vanish |
| Sport-specific keypoints: ski tip, club head, racket face, bar ends, bat | **Yes** | Add keypoints to a pose model, or a separate small keypoint/detector model | No off-the-shelf model has these points |
| Phase or action recognition (serve vs volley, takeoff, entry) | **Sometimes** | Usually a small model on keypoint sequences; rarely a video model | Rule-based phases (velocity minima, contact events) work for repetitive motions; learned models help for varied actions |
| Holistic quality score ("how good was this dive") | **Yes, plus lots of data** | AQA model (video or pose based) | Judge scores need 1,000+ scored attempts to learn (see 5.3) |
| Team sports: same player across occlusions and cuts | **Yes** | ReID embedding for tracker; jersey-number OCR | Generic ReID confuses players in identical kits |
| Court/field/course registration | **Yes, once per sport** | Field-keypoint or line model | Each sport's template differs; SoccerNet-style calibration models are sport-specific ([SoccerNet-GSR](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11335v1)) |

### 3.2 Which model families, as of late 2026

| Task | Default choice | Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detection | **YOLO11 / YOLO26** (Ultralytics) | **RF-DETR** (Apache-2.0, DINOv2 backbone) | YOLO26 (Sept 2025) is NMS-free and covers detect/seg/pose/OBB ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25164v2)). RF-DETR is the first real-time model above 60 AP on COCO, with an ICLR 2026 paper and a segmentation variant since Jan 2026 ([Roboflow](https://playground.roboflow.com/models/roboflow/rf-detr)). **Ultralytics is AGPL-3.0**, so commercial use needs a licence; RF-DETR doesn't. Test both on tiny objects; our 8–15 px buoy case is the hard one for DETRs. |
| 2D pose | **YOLO11/26-pose** (fast, top-down free) | **RTMPose / RTMO via [rtmlib](https://github.com/Tau-J/rtmlib)** (Apache, ONNX only), **ViTPose** (Apache, 81.1 AP COCO) | YOLO-pose won on our skier footage. RTMPose is the easiest to fine-tune with extra keypoints. |
| High-fidelity pose / dense | Sapiens / Sapiens2 | – | Very accurate, heavy (up to 5B parameters), **non-commercial licence (CC BY-NC)** for v1; check the Sapiens2 licence ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/sapiens/blob/5edcfe85b0d6b7137b5e351f44edf4e9303961eb/README.md?code=true)). Use it as an offline labeller, not in production. |
| Segmentation / video masks | **SAM 3** (concept prompts, video tracking, Nov 2025) | SAM 2.1 | SAM 3 finds *all* instances of a text concept and tracks them ([Ultralytics docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/sam-3), [Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/what-is-sam3/)). It is our best candidate for auto-labelling. Too slow for per-frame production. |
| Open-vocab labelling | SAM 3, Grounding DINO, OWLv2, Florence-2 | – | Labellers, not runtime models (our measurement). |
| Action / phase on keypoints | **1D-CNN / GRU / TCN** on L3 features; ST-GCN-style | – | Trains in minutes on CPU with a few hundred segments. |
| Action on pixels | VideoMAE v2, InternVideo2 | TimeSformer | Only when the cue isn't in the skeleton (ball spin, splash size). Needs GPUs and thousands of clips. |
| Tracking | ByteTrack / BoT-SORT with camera-motion compensation | OC-SORT; DeepStream NvDCF | Add a fine-tuned ReID head only for team sports. |

Note on OpenMMLab (MMPose/MMAction2): third-party health trackers rate these repos as low-activity or
inactive since 2024–25 ([Snyk mmpose](https://snyk.io/advisor/python/mmpose),
[Snyk mmaction](https://snyk.io/advisor/python/mmaction)). Use their **models** (exported to ONNX via
rtmlib), but don't build the platform on their training framework.

### 3.3 How much data, and how to get it cheaply

| Fine-tune target | Typical data to be useful | Our evidence / rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| Detector for 1–3 new object classes | 500–2,000 labelled frames from 10+ videos covering lighting, venues and cameras | Our 1,608 auto-labelled frames gave AP50 ~0.80, **but generalised poorly to an unseen lake**. Venue diversity matters more than frame count. |
| Pose fine-tune for unusual viewpoints | 1,000–3,000 annotated persons | Start from COCO weights; add a few hundred of the hardest poses. |
| Extra sport keypoints (ski tip, club head) | 300–1,000 frames | Auto-label from a detector box plus manual click. |
| Keypoint-sequence classifier | 100–300 examples per class | Balance faulty and clean reps; split by athlete, not by clip. |
| Holistic AQA regressor | 1,000+ scored attempts | MTL-AQA uses ~1,059 training samples; FineDiving ~2,250 ([AQA survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.11149)). |

**Auto-labelling loop (generic, reuse ours):** (1) Prompt SAM 3 or Grounding DINO with the object name;
the name comes from the user or a VLM. (2) Clean with temporal consistency and size/colour priors, which
is what `tools/clean_dataset.py` does and removed ~38 % noise. (3) A human reviews a gallery, which is
fast: accept/reject, not drawing. (4) Train a small detector. (5) Run it on new footage and queue its
low-confidence frames for review (active learning). Roboflow's
[Autodistill](https://inference.roboflow.com/quickstart/roboflow_ecosystem/) packages the same idea.

## 4. Evaluation: proving the scores are right

### 4.1 What we measure, per layer

| Level | Metric | Ground truth needed |
|---|---|---|
| Perception | AP50 / recall for objects; PCK / OKS for keypoints; IDF1 / HOTA for tracks; "% frames usable" | 200–500 labelled frames per sport |
| Events | Per-event precision / recall / F1 with time tolerance (e.g. ±0.2 s); **coverage** (% events decided vs unknown) | event timestamps on 30–100 clips |
| Criteria (binary) | Accuracy, F1, **Cohen's kappa** vs expert (kappa corrects for chance; > 0.6 is "substantial") | expert pass/fail on each criterion |
| Scores (continuous) | **Spearman rank correlation** vs judges (the AQA standard), MAE in score points, % within ±N points, **ICC** for agreement | judge or expert scores |
| Confidence | Calibration: reliability diagram, ECE. "When we say 90 % sure, are we right 90 % of the time?" | same as above |
| Human baseline | Inter-rater agreement between two experts | double-labelled subset |

Always report **expert-vs-expert agreement next to model-vs-expert**. If two coaches only reach kappa
0.5 on "knees caving", the model cannot be expected to do better, and the product claim should say so.
Even in the official Functional Movement Screen, scored 0–3 per test, inter-rater reliability is a
recurring research topic ([IJSPT](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/74724-the-functional-movement-screen-exploring-interrater-)).

### 4.2 Building gold sets fast

1. **Use free ground truth where it exists:** official results (we used scores in file names for slalom),
   competition score sheets, broadcast graphics, and rep counts announced by the athlete. These are weak
   labels at run level, but they cost nothing.
2. **Label events, not frames.** A tool that plays the clip and lets an expert press keys
   (`rep bottom`, `fault`, `buoy L outside`) gives ~1 minute of labelling per clip.
3. **Sample for diversity:** stratify by venue, camera, athlete and outcome. 50 well-chosen clips beat
   500 from one session.
4. **Freeze a test set per sport** and run it in CI. Every rule change and model change shows its delta.
5. **Active learning:** route `Unknown` results, low-confidence results and model/rule disagreements to
   the label queue. Roboflow Workflows ships an
   ["active learning data collector" block](https://roboflow.com/build-a-workflow/roboflow-classification-model-to-active-learning-data-collector)
   that does this for detections; we need the same at event level.

**Pattern:** every successful company is either *one sport done deeply* or *fixed hardware*. Nobody
sells "define any sport, get a judge". That is both the opportunity and the warning. The generic part
is real, but each sport pack needs domain-expert time.

### 5.3 What action-quality-assessment (AQA) research says

- **Datasets:** AQA-7 (7 Olympic events), MTL-AQA (~1.4k dives with judge scores and commentary),
  FineDiving (3k dives with sub-action steps; [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03646)), FineGym
  (fine-grained gymnastics action labels), Fitness-AQA (squat/row/press errors labelled by trainers,
  non-commercial; [ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136980104.pdf)),
  FineFS (figure skating GOE). The 2024 survey covers 150+ papers
  ([arXiv 2412.11149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.11149)).
- **Headline numbers look great:** Spearman ≈ 0.95–0.96 vs judges on MTL-AQA/FineDiving (CoRe 0.951,
  FineParser 0.959; [FineParser](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06887)).
- **But read them carefully:**
  - These datasets are single-sport, from fixed broadcast angles, with a known difficulty degree
    multiplied in. Much of the correlation comes from **difficulty, which is known in advance**, not
    from execution.
  - Rank correlation over a whole competition hides large per-attempt errors.
  - Models don't transfer across sports without retraining.
- **What works best:** methods that **parse the action into steps or phases, then compare against
  references** (FineDiving's procedure-aware approach, contrastive regression against exemplar
  attempts), and pose-guided methods
  ([hierarchical pose-guided AQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03674)).

  This supports our architecture: phases plus per-phase criteria plus a comparison to reference
  attempts. A cheap platform version of "contrastive regression" is to **compare a rep's feature
  curves to a small library of expert-scored reference reps** (DTW distance or kNN on embeddings).
  That gives a score with no deep training and only a handful of references.
- **General VLMs are not judges yet** (see Section 2): near-chance AQA in 2026, even with skeleton
  hints or in-context examples ([summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.08294)).
  Specially trained video judges exist ([VideoJudge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21451)) but target
  generic video QA, not sports scoring.

## 6. Recommended architecture and MVP plan

### 6.1 Tech choices

| Concern | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Language/runtime | Python 3.11, ONNX Runtime / TensorRT | Reuse existing code; single export path |
| Perception | YOLO11/26 (detect+pose) **behind an interface**, RF-DETR as the licence-safe option; rtmlib RTMPose as a second pose backend; SAM 3 offline only | Swappable backends; licence decision deferred but possible |
| Tracking | ByteTrack/BoT-SORT plus our camera-motion compensation (`cvdemo/camera.py`) | Proven on slalom |
| Representation (L2) | Arrow/Parquet tables: `frames`, `tracks`, `keypoints`, `objects`, `camera`, `events`; one schema, versioned | Cheap re-scoring without re-running models (we already do this with `rescore.py`) |
| Features/rules (L3/L4) | Pure-Python library plus `@criterion` decorator; rules run in a sandbox; NumPy only | Testable, LLM-friendly |
| LLM authoring | Claude (or another frontier LLM) with the feature API docs plus 3–5 example rules in the prompt; tool loop that runs the rule on labelled clips and returns a confusion matrix | The "C on top of A" loop from 2.3 |
| Labelling | CVAT or Label Studio for boxes/keypoints; a small custom event-tagging web page for clips | Event labels are the scarce resource |
| Orchestration | Batch jobs (one video = one job) on a GPU queue; DeepStream later for live/multi-stream | Simple first |
| Sport pack | Folder: `pack.yaml` (objects, keypoints, scene template, phases), `criteria/*.py`, `models/`, `gold/`, `report.jinja` | Adding a sport = adding a folder |

### 6.2 MVP: three sports chosen to stress different parts

| Sport | Stresses | Training needed | Ground truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Squat (or push-up) form** | Pose-only rules, rep segmentation, LLM rule authoring, per-rep 0–100 score | None | Record 100–200 reps ourselves; a coach labels faults (depth, knees in, back angle, heel lift) |
| **Water-ski slalom** (port) | Small-object detector, camera motion, object–person relations, official-score comparison | Buoy detector (exists) | Official scores (exists) plus per-buoy labels on 15 videos |
| **Tennis serve or basketball free throw** | Ball detector, contact/release events, trajectory, one phase model | Ball detector (auto-labelled) | Made/missed is free ground truth for free throws; a coach rates serve mechanics |

**Success criterion for "generic":** sport 3 should take less than half the engineering time of the
slalom port, and most of that time should go into the pack folder, not into core code.

### 6.3 Plan and effort (2–3 engineers)

| Weeks | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | L2 schema and storage; refactor slalom into core + pack; re-scoring from stored tracks; CI with a frozen slalom gold set |

Rough total: **10–14 weeks** to a convincing three-sport demo with honest metrics. Adding each further
sport afterwards: **1–2 weeks** for a pose-only activity, **3–6 weeks** for one needing a new object
detector and scene geometry, plus the expert's time for labels.

### 6.4 Hardware and running cost

Measured on our pipeline: ~15 s of 4-core CPU or ~2 s of T4 GPU per second of video.

| Setup | Compute per minute of video | Approx. cloud cost per minute of video |
|---|---|---|
| T4 GPU (~$0.35–0.55/h on-demand) | ~2 min GPU | **~$0.012–0.02** |
| 4-core CPU (~$0.15/h) | ~15 min CPU | ~$0.04, but 15× slower than real time |
| T4 with TensorRT FP16 + batching (DeepStream) | expect 2–4× better | <$0.01 |
| VLM per clip (if used for feedback text) | – | cents per clip; dominated by token count |

Pose-only activities are much cheaper: one detector plus one pose model, no high-resolution small-object
pass. Even CPU or phone (MediaPipe/RTMPose) is viable. High-resolution small-object detection
(1280 px, our buoys) is the expensive part.

### 6.5 Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Each sport's geometry is bespoke (as on slalom) | "Platform" turns into a set of one-offs | Measure engineering time per pack; invest in the geometry toolkit; pick sport 3 to prove reuse |
| No ground truth, so scores are unverifiable | Users lose trust | Gold set per sport is a hard requirement before a score is shown; show "uncalibrated" otherwise |
| Visibility gaps (spray, occlusion, off-screen) | Many `Unknown`s | Report coverage honestly; it's often better to say "not seen" than to guess |
| Camera variety (phone vs broadcast vs fixed) | Thresholds don't transfer | Normalise by body size; capture guidelines per pack; test on several venues |
| Licences (Ultralytics AGPL, Sapiens NC, Fitness-AQA NC, third-party footage) | Blocks commercial use | Keep backends swappable; RF-DETR + RTMPose/ViTPose (Apache) path ready |
| LLM-generated rules that are subtly wrong | Silent errors | Rules only go live after passing the gold set; humans review the code; rules are versioned |
| Expectation of "judge-level 0–100 for any sport" | Overpromising | Sell explainable criteria first; holistic scores only where scored data exists |

## 7. Plain answer: do we need to fine-tune models? Which, how, why?

**Often no, sometimes yes, and it's cheaper than it sounds.**

- **Person detection and body skeleton: usually no.** Models trained on everyday photos (COCO) already
  find people and their joints well when the person is upright and reasonably large in the frame. That
  covers gym exercises, running and most court sports. Our slalom skier and skeleton worked off the
  shelf.
- **Small or unusual sport objects: yes.** Balls, buoys, pucks, rackets, bars and gates are tiny, blurred
  or simply not in those training sets. General "find anything by name" models are too slow and miss
  tiny objects. The fix: let a big model label a few thousand frames automatically, have a person
  clean them up in a few hours, and train a small fast detector (YOLO or RF-DETR) in an hour. That is
  what we did for buoys. The main lesson: **include footage from many venues**, or it won't work on a
  new lake.
- **Skeleton in unusual situations: sometimes.** Upside-down gymnasts, swimmers, skiers in spray,
  divers in a tuck. Fine-tune the pose model (RTMPose, ViTPose or YOLO-pose) on a few hundred to a few
  thousand hard examples. The same applies to extra points like a ski tip or a club head, which no
  standard model knows.
- **Tracking: rarely the model; usually the camera.** Our tracking problems came from the camera
  panning, and we fixed them with motion compensation, not training. Fine-tune a re-identification
  model only for team sports where players in the same kit must be kept apart.
- **Judging "correct vs wrong": prefer rules, not training.** If you can say it ("arm straight at
  release"), write it as a rule. An LLM can draft the rule and a few labelled clips can verify it. Train
  a small classifier on skeleton movement only when experts can spot a fault but cannot describe it,
  and you have a few hundred labelled examples.
- **A single holistic "how good was it" score: yes, and it needs a lot of judged data.** Research
  systems reach strong agreement with diving judges, but only with thousands of scored attempts from
  one sport and one camera set-up. Without that data, build the score from explainable criteria, or
  compare each attempt to a handful of reference attempts scored by an expert.
- **Large vision-language models as judges: not yet.** They are useful helpers for labelling, rule
  drafting and written feedback, but they score sports technique barely better than chance today.
